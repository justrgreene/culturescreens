02 / THE STORY

Our everyday narratives hold maps of where we’ve been—and where we could go.

FIELD
NOTES02

Essay / 06 minute read — 02

A story is
a place to meet.

A story can be a thread between people who have never met. It can make the faraway feel close—and help the familiar look new.

We tell stories to remember, to imagine, to make sense of what happens next. They travel in books and films, in family lore, in the sentence someone chooses to post. Every retelling carries a little change: a new detail, a new voice, a new listener.

That’s why representation matters. The stories that get repeated can become the edges of what seems possible. Making room for more voices isn’t just adding to the collection; it changes the shape of the whole picture.

“Who gets to be ordinary in the stories we tell?”A QUESTION WORTH KEEPING

Listen for the missing voice

Next time you meet a familiar plot, ask who gets to speak and who gets to decide what the ending means. Then look for the stories that make room for a different answer.
